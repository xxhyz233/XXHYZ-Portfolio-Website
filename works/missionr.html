Porsche Mission R

Brief.

Porsche Mission R is a personal CG short film that reimagines Porsche’s electric concept race car with exposed carbon-fiber bodywork and a custom livery designed for PG, a fictional racing team. Moving from the stillness of the garage to the intensity of the racetrack, the film explores the car’s contrasting character at rest and in motion.

Process.

The entire vehicle was modeled from scratch, using Blender for initial blocking, geometry refinement, and UV unwrapping, alongside Plasticity for precision hard-surface modeling and intricate mechanical details.

Texturing and material development were carried out in Substance Painter, exploring various livery designs and material combinations across metallic finishes, glass, and carbon fiber. The final design features a metallic livery layered over a glossy carbon-fiber body, emphasizing the contrast between graphic elements and the underlying material.

The overall design direction preserves the original visual identity of Porsche’s electric concept race car while introducing a distinctive aesthetic aligned with the fictional PG racing team.

Motion.

The CG short extends an automotive design project into motion and sound. I began by designing a world for the racing team and modeling the car down to its chassis and electric powertrain. The film follows the car around the circuit, through a tunnel, and into the garage, with close-ups highlighting the design of the powertrain, wheels, wings, and diffusers.

The concept of “cruise” guided the framing, camera movement, and pacing. In the rain sequence, I used the car’s low silhouette and carbon-fiber finish to shape how it reads against the wet track. The shots emphasize the car’s composure at speed as it moves through the downpour.

I designed the graphical interface around a grid that responds to the car’s speed. Each cell expands as the car accelerates and contracts as it slows, giving the interface a breathing rhythm. I completed all animation, rigging, and simulation in Cinema 4D, rendered the film with Octane, and edited it in After Effects.
